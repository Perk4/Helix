# NONCLINICAL STUDY REPORT

**Study Title:** A 28-Day Repeat-Dose Oral Gavage Toxicity Study with Compound BM-341 in Sprague-Dawley Rats
**Study Number:** TOX-2024-0931
**Test Facility:** Nonclinical Safety Research, New Brunswick, NJ
**Sponsor:** Internal — Nonclinical Safety Research
**Test Article:** Compound BM-341
**Species/Strain:** Rat / Sprague-Dawley (Crl:CD(SD))
**GLP Status:** GLP
**Study Director:** R. Patel, PhD
**Report Date:** 04 October 2024
**Report Status:** APPROVED

---

## Summary

Compound BM-341 was administered once daily by oral gavage to Sprague-Dawley rats (5/sex/group) at doses of 0, 15, 75, or 300 mg/kg/day for 28 consecutive days. All 40 animals survived to scheduled necropsy. No treatment-related clinical signs were observed at any dose level.

No treatment-related effects on body weight, food consumption, or organ weights were observed at 15 or 75 mg/kg/day. At 300 mg/kg/day, a very slight and non-statistically-significant increase in kidney weight was noted in males (+5.8% vs. controls) and females (+5.2% vs. controls), in the absence of any adverse clinical, macroscopic, or functional correlates.

Microscopically, minimal renal tubular basophilia (Grade 1) was observed in 3 of 5 Group 3 males (75 mg/kg/day) and in all Group 4 animals (300 mg/kg/day). At 300 mg/kg/day, minimal focal erythroid hyperplasia (Grade 1) was observed in the bone marrow of 3 of 5 males and 2 of 5 females. These findings are characterised by basophilic change in proximal tubular epithelium consistent with enzyme induction, and an increase in erythroid precursor cells in the bone marrow consistent with haematopoietic stimulation, respectively. Both findings are considered adaptive physiological responses at the cellular level, with no associated adverse functional, clinical, or gross pathological correlates. No compound-related adverse findings were identified at any dose level.

**The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) for Compound BM-341 under the conditions of this study is 300 mg/kg/day**, the highest dose tested. No lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL) was established.

---

## 1. Objective

The objectives of this study were to: (1) evaluate the potential toxicity of Compound BM-341 following repeated oral gavage administration to Sprague-Dawley rats for 28 consecutive days; (2) characterise the nature, severity, and dose-response relationship of any treatment-related effects; (3) identify any target organs of toxicity; and (4) establish a no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) to support further development. The study was conducted in compliance with Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) regulations.

Compound BM-341 is a novel small-molecule inhibitor of a renal proximal tubular transporter under development for the treatment of metabolic syndrome. Based on the mechanism of action and the renal tubular target, the kidney was designated as the organ of primary interest. Bone marrow was identified as a secondary organ of interest based on in vitro haematopoietic assay data. The doses of 15, 75, and 300 mg/kg/day were selected based on the no-effect level and supratherapeutic multiples established in a 7-day dose range-finding study, in which no adverse findings were observed up to 300 mg/kg/day.

## 3. Materials and Methods

### 3.1 Test Article

Compound BM-341 was supplied by the Sponsor Analytical Chemistry Group. The batch used (BM341-2024-D02) had a purity of 98.3% (HPLC). The test article was stored refrigerated (2 to 8°C), protected from light.

### 3.2 Vehicle

Vehicle was sterile saline (0.9% NaCl, w/v). Dosing formulations were prepared as solutions and stored refrigerated (2 to 8°C).

### 3.3 Test System

Naive, virus antibody-free Sprague-Dawley rats (Crl:CD(SD)) were obtained from Charles River Laboratories. Animals arrived at 7 weeks of age and were acclimated for a minimum of 5 days prior to Day 1. Animals were assigned to groups by stratified randomisation based on Day 1 body weight.

### 3.4 Dose Formulation Verification

Dosing formulation samples were collected for concentration verification on Days 1 and 28. Samples were analysed by HPLC. Acceptance criteria: measured concentration within ±10% of nominal.

### 3.5 Terminal Procedures

All surviving animals were euthanised on Day 29 by carbon dioxide inhalation followed by exsanguination. A complete macroscopic examination was performed on all animals. Terminal body weights were recorded immediately prior to euthanasia. The following organs were weighed: liver, kidneys (paired), heart, brain, spleen, and thymus.

### 3.6 Histology and Histopathology

Tissues from all animals in Groups 1 and 4 were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, processed, and embedded in paraffin. Target tissues (kidneys and bone marrow) from Groups 2 and 3 were similarly processed. Sections were cut at 4 to 6 μm and stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Microscopic findings were graded on a scale of 1 (Minimal) to 5 (Severe). A pathology peer review was conducted by an independent board-certified veterinary pathologist.

### 3.7 Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables (body weights, organ weights) were analysed by one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post-hoc test. Where Bartlett's test indicated heterogeneity of variance (p < 0.05), the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn's post-hoc was applied. Incidence data were compared by Fisher's exact test. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Test Article Formulation and Analyses Verification

Dosing formulation samples were collected and analysed on Days 1 and 28. All measured concentrations were within ±10% of the nominal target concentration. No test article was detected in vehicle control formulations. All dosing formulations met the predefined acceptance criteria.

### 5.2 In-Life Observations and Measurements

#### 5.2.1 Mortality

No unscheduled deaths or moribund euthanasias occurred during the study. All 40 animals (20 males, 20 females) survived to scheduled necropsy on Day 29.

#### 5.2.2 Clinical Observations

Cage-side observations were recorded twice daily throughout the study. Detailed clinical examinations were performed weekly.

**Group 1 (0 mg/kg/day — Vehicle Control):** No treatment-related clinical signs were observed in any male or female animal during the study.

**Group 2 (15 mg/kg/day — Low Dose):** No treatment-related clinical signs were observed. All animals appeared healthy and clinically normal throughout the dosing period.

**Group 3 (75 mg/kg/day — Mid Dose):** No treatment-related clinical signs were observed. All animals appeared healthy and clinically normal throughout the dosing period.

**Group 4 (300 mg/kg/day — High Dose):** No treatment-related clinical signs were observed at any cage-side or detailed clinical examination timepoint. All Group 4 animals appeared healthy and clinically normal throughout the 28-day dosing period.

#### 5.3.2 Macroscopic Observations

A complete macroscopic examination was performed on all animals at scheduled necropsy on Day 29.

**Groups 1, 2, 3, and 4 (0, 15, 75, and 300 mg/kg/day):** No treatment-related macroscopic findings were observed in any tissue, including the kidneys, liver, spleen, thymus, adrenal glands, lungs, heart, brain, or gonads, in any male or female animal at any dose level. All tissues examined appeared grossly normal. The minimal increase in kidney weight observed in Group 4 animals was not associated with any macroscopic correlate.

#### 5.3.3 Microscopic Findings

Microscopic examination was performed on all tissues from Group 1 and Group 4 animals, and on target tissues (kidneys and bone marrow) from Groups 2 and 3.

**Kidneys:**

Group 1: No treatment-related microscopic findings. Renal parenchyma was within normal limits in all animals examined.

Group 2 (15 mg/kg/day): No treatment-related renal microscopic findings. All kidney sections appeared within normal limits.

Group 3 (75 mg/kg/day): Minimal renal tubular basophilia (Grade 1) was observed in 3 of 5 males (M301, M303, M304). The finding was characterised by increased cytoplasmic basophilia of proximal tubular epithelial cells in the cortex, consistent with increased ribosomal content and protein synthetic activity associated with enzyme induction. No tubular degeneration, necrosis, cast formation, or inflammatory infiltrate was observed. This change is considered an adaptive cellular response to the pharmacological activity of BM-341 at the proximal tubular transporter, and is not considered adverse.

Group 4 (300 mg/kg/day): Minimal to mild renal tubular basophilia (Grade 1 to 2) was observed in all 5 males and all 5 females. The morphological characteristics were consistent with those described in Group 3, with slightly greater severity in Group 4. No tubular degeneration, necrosis, tubular dilation, cast formation, or interstitial inflammation was observed in any Group 4 animal. The very slight kidney weight increase in Group 4 is considered attributable to this adaptive cellular response. In the absence of evidence of tubular injury, impaired renal function, or any other adverse correlate, renal tubular basophilia at 300 mg/kg/day is considered an adaptive, non-adverse finding.

**Bone Marrow:**

Groups 1, 2, and 3: No treatment-related bone marrow findings in any animal.

Group 4 (300 mg/kg/day): Minimal focal erythroid hyperplasia (Grade 1) was observed in 3 of 5 males and 2 of 5 females. The finding was characterised by a mild local increase in erythroid precursor cells within the femoral marrow space. Overall marrow cellularity was maintained. The finding is considered a compensatory haematopoietic response and, in the absence of anaemia, haemolysis, haematological abnormalities, or reduction in overall marrow cellularity, is classified as an adaptive, non-adverse response.

**Liver:**

No treatment-related microscopic findings were identified in the liver in any dose group. Hepatic parenchyma appeared within normal limits in all Group 1 and Group 4 animals examined.

**Other Organs (Heart, Brain, Spleen, Thymus, Adrenal Glands, Gonads, Lungs):**

No treatment-related microscopic findings were identified in the heart, brain, spleen, thymus, adrenal glands, gonads, or lungs in any dose group.

#### 5.3.4 Conclusion

No adverse treatment-related findings were identified at any dose level tested in this study. The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) for Compound BM-341 under the conditions of this study is **300 mg/kg/day**, the highest dose tested. No lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL) was established.

The minimal to mild renal tubular basophilia observed at 75 and 300 mg/kg/day is considered an adaptive cellular response to the pharmacological activity of BM-341 at the proximal tubular transporter target. This finding is not considered adverse based on: (1) absence of tubular degeneration or necrosis; (2) absence of cast formation or inflammatory infiltrate; (3) no adverse macroscopic renal correlate; (4) no statistically significant organ weight change; and (5) consistency with a well-characterised adaptive response to transporter inhibition in this species.

The minimal focal erythroid hyperplasia observed in the bone marrow of Group 4 animals is considered a compensatory haematopoietic response and is not adverse in the context of maintained overall marrow cellularity and the absence of haematological abnormalities.
