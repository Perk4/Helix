# NONCLINICAL STUDY REPORT

**Study Title:** A 13-Week Repeat-Dose Oral Gavage Toxicity Study with Compound LF-892 in Sprague-Dawley Rats with a 4-Week Recovery Period
**Study Number:** TOX-2023-0856
**Test Facility:** Nonclinical Safety Research, New Brunswick, NJ
**Sponsor:** Internal — Nonclinical Safety Research
**Test Article:** Compound LF-892
**Species/Strain:** Rat / Sprague-Dawley (Crl:CD(SD))
**GLP Status:** GLP
**Study Director:** M. Chen, DVM, PhD
**Report Date:** 22 September 2023
**Report Status:** APPROVED

---

## Summary

Compound LF-892 was administered once daily by oral gavage to Sprague-Dawley rats (10/sex/group main; 5/sex/group recovery for Groups 1 and 4) at doses of 0, 10, 50, or 150 mg/kg/day for 13 consecutive weeks, followed by a 4-week recovery period for designated animals. One Group 1 male (M101) died following an inadvertent gavage error on Day 35; this death was considered non-treatment-related. No treatment-related deaths occurred during the study.

At 10 mg/kg/day, minimal intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation (Grade 1) was observed in 2 of 10 males at the lung. This finding was considered incidental and non-adverse based on its low incidence and minimal severity. All other parameters were unaffected.

At 50 mg/kg/day, intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation (Grade 1) was observed in all 10 males and 6 of 10 females at the lung, and was considered treatment-related and adverse. Mild hepatocellular vacuolisation (Grade 1) was present in 3 of 10 males. Body weight gain was mildly reduced in males (−7.3% vs. controls at Week 13) and females (−4.7% vs. controls at Week 13). Lung weights were slightly increased.

At 150 mg/kg/day, marked clinical signs were present throughout the study including reduced activity, piloerection, and laboured breathing in multiple animals. Body weight gain was markedly reduced in males (−15.0%) and females (−9.8%) by Week 13. Lung weights were substantially increased in all Group 4 animals. Grossly, lungs appeared dark red and consolidated in most Group 4 animals. Microscopically, moderate to marked intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation (Grade 2–3), pulmonary inflammation (Grade 2), and perivascular lymphoid infiltration (Grade 1) were observed in all Group 4 animals. Hepatocellular vacuolisation (Grade 1–2) was present in all Group 4 males and most Group 4 females. Thymic lymphoid depletion (Grade 1–2) was observed in the majority of Group 4 animals. All findings at 150 mg/kg/day are considered adverse. Following the 4-week recovery period, lung macrophage accumulation was partially reversible (Grade 1 residual), hepatocellular vacuolisation was fully reversible, and body weights recovered toward control values.

**The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) for Compound LF-892 under the conditions of this study is 10 mg/kg/day.** The lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL) is 50 mg/kg/day, based on treatment-related pulmonary macrophage accumulation in all animals at that dose level.

---

## 1. Objective

The objectives of this study were to: (1) evaluate the potential subchronic toxicity of Compound LF-892 following repeated oral gavage administration to Sprague-Dawley rats for 13 consecutive weeks; (2) characterise the nature, severity, dose-response relationship, and reversibility of any treatment-related effects; (3) identify target organs of toxicity; and (4) establish a no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) to support regulatory submission. The study was conducted in compliance with Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) regulations.

Compound LF-892 is a small-molecule candidate under development for treatment of pulmonary inflammatory disease. Based on the pharmacological mechanism of action (inhibition of alveolar macrophage activation) and repeat-dose range-finding data, the lung was identified as the primary target organ. A 4-week recovery cohort was included to assess the reversibility of any identified findings.

## 3. Materials and Methods

### 3.1 Test Article

Compound LF-892 was supplied by the Sponsor Analytical Chemistry Group. The batch used (LF892-2022-C03) had a purity of 99.1% (HPLC). The test article was stored at controlled room temperature, protected from light.

### 3.2 Vehicle

Vehicle was 0.5% methylcellulose (w/v) in purified water. Dosing formulations were prepared as a suspension weekly and stored refrigerated (2 to 8°C).

### 3.3 Test System

Naive, virus antibody-free Sprague-Dawley rats (Crl:CD(SD)) were obtained from Charles River Laboratories. Animals arrived at 7 weeks of age and were acclimated for a minimum of 5 days prior to Day 1. Animals were assigned to groups by stratified randomisation based on Day 1 body weight.

### 3.4 Dose Formulation Verification

Dosing formulation samples were collected for concentration verification on Day 1 and during Week 13. Samples were analysed by HPLC. Acceptance criteria: measured concentration within ±10% of nominal.

### 3.5 Terminal Procedures

Main group animals were euthanised at Day 92 (Week 13 + 1 day). Recovery group animals were euthanised at Day 120 (4 weeks post-treatment). All animals were euthanised by carbon dioxide inhalation followed by exsanguination. A complete macroscopic post-mortem examination was performed on all animals. The following organs were weighed: liver, lungs, kidneys (paired), heart, brain, spleen, and thymus.

### 3.6 Histology and Histopathology

Tissues from all animals in Groups 1 and 4 (and their respective recovery groups) and target tissues (lungs, liver) from Groups 2 and 3 were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, processed, and embedded in paraffin. Sections were cut at 4 to 6 μm and stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Microscopic findings were graded on a scale of 1 (Minimal) to 5 (Severe). A pathology peer review was conducted by an independent board-certified veterinary pathologist.

### 3.7 Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables (body weights, organ weights) were analysed by one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post-hoc test. Where Bartlett's test indicated heterogeneity of variance (p < 0.05), the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn's post-hoc was applied. Incidence data were compared by Fisher's exact test. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

---

## 4. Deviations

One unscheduled death occurred during the study. Group 1 male M101 was found moribund on Day 35 and was euthanised by carbon dioxide inhalation. Gross pathology revealed oesophageal perforation consistent with inadvertent gavage error. This death was classified as accidental and non-treatment-related. The event was documented as a protocol deviation; no impact on study integrity was identified.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Test Article Formulation and Analyses Verification

Dosing formulation samples were collected and analysed on Day 1 and during Week 13. All measured concentrations were within ±10% of the nominal target concentration. No test article was detected in vehicle control formulations. All dosing formulations met the predefined acceptance criteria.

### 5.2 In-Life Observations and Measurements

#### 5.2.1 Mortality

One unscheduled death occurred during the study period. Group 1 male M101 was found moribund on Day 35 and was euthanised. Gross post-mortem examination revealed oesophageal perforation, consistent with an inadvertent gavage error. This death was considered accidental and non-treatment-related. All remaining 99 animals (39 males, 40 females, and 20 recovery animals across Groups 1 and 4) survived to scheduled necropsy. No treatment-related deaths occurred during the 13-week treatment period or the 4-week recovery period.

#### 5.2.2 Clinical Observations

Cage-side observations were recorded twice daily throughout the study. Detailed clinical examinations were performed weekly.

**Group 1 (0 mg/kg/day — Vehicle Control):** No treatment-related clinical signs were observed. The death of M101 on Day 35 was associated with clinical signs of respiratory distress immediately prior to moribund sacrifice, consistent with gavage error.

**Group 2 (10 mg/kg/day — Low Dose):** No treatment-related clinical signs were observed. All animals appeared healthy throughout the dosing period.

**Group 3 (50 mg/kg/day — Mid Dose):** Occasional soft stool was noted in 4 of 20 animals (M303, M305, F301, F308) during Days 1 to 14. The finding was transient and resolved without intervention. No other clinical signs were noted.

**Group 4 (150 mg/kg/day — High Dose):** Multiple clinical signs consistent with pulmonary and systemic toxicity were observed from Week 2 onwards. Reduced activity was observed in 12 of 20 main group animals from Days 8 to 91. Piloerection was noted in 9 of 20 animals from Days 8 to 63. Laboured breathing was observed in 6 of 20 animals (M401, M402, M404, F401, F403, F405) from Days 15 to 91. Reduced food consumption was recorded in most Group 4 animals throughout the dosing period. All clinical signs were considered treatment-related and resolved during the recovery period. By Week 4 of recovery, no residual clinical signs were observed in any Group 4 recovery animal.

During the 4-week recovery period, Group 4 males recovered to 408 g (94.7% of control) and Group 4 females to 268 g (96.1% of control), indicating substantial but not complete recovery of body weight gain.

#### 5.3.2 Macroscopic Observations

A complete macroscopic examination was performed on all animals at scheduled necropsy and recovery necropsy.

**Groups 1 and 2 (0 and 10 mg/kg/day):** No treatment-related macroscopic findings were observed in any tissue. The oesophageal perforation in M101 was confirmed at gross necropsy as the cause of death.

**Group 3 (50 mg/kg/day):** No macroscopic findings attributable to treatment were identified in the lungs or any other tissue. The microscopic lung changes at this dose level were not associated with a macroscopic correlate.

**Group 4 (150 mg/kg/day):** The lungs appeared dark red and exhibited multifocal areas of consolidation in 8 of 10 males and 7 of 10 females. Lung lobes were firm on palpation in the most severely affected animals (M401, M402, F401, F403). The thymus appeared small in 6 of 10 animals. No macroscopic liver findings were noted despite the presence of microscopic hepatocellular vacuolisation. No macroscopic findings were noted in the kidneys, heart, brain, spleen, or gonads in any group.

**Recovery (Groups 1R and 4R):** No macroscopic findings were observed in Group 1R animals. Residual mild lung consolidation was noted in 2 of 5 Group 4R males (M401R, M403R) and 1 of 5 Group 4R females (F401R), indicating partial macroscopic reversibility. All other tissues were unremarkable in recovery animals.

#### 5.3.3 Microscopic Findings

Microscopic examination was performed on tissues from all animals in Groups 1 and 4 and their respective recovery groups, and on target tissues (lungs, liver) from Groups 2 and 3.

**Lungs:**

Groups 1 and 2: Group 1 animals showed no treatment-related pulmonary findings. In Group 2, minimal intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation (Grade 1) was observed in 2 of 10 males (M201, M207). The finding was characterised by scattered clusters of foamy macrophages within alveolar spaces without accompanying inflammation. This finding was considered incidental in nature based on low incidence and absence of associated inflammatory changes, and was not classified as adverse.

Group 3: Intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation (Grade 1) was present in all 10 males and 6 of 10 females. Macrophages were foamy in appearance and distributed in a multifocal pattern. The higher incidence compared to Group 2 indicates a treatment-related effect at this dose; the finding is considered adverse based on its occurrence in all Group 3 males.

Group 4: Moderate to marked intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation (Grade 2 to 3) was present in all 10 males and all 10 females. Additionally, pulmonary inflammation characterised by neutrophilic and lymphocytic infiltration (Grade 2) was observed in 7 of 10 males and 6 of 10 females. Perivascular lymphoid infiltration (Grade 1) was noted in 5 of 10 animals. All pulmonary findings are considered adverse and directly attributable to compound administration.

Group 4R (Recovery): Residual Grade 1 intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation was present in 3 of 5 Group 4R males and 2 of 5 Group 4R females, indicating partial reversibility. Pulmonary inflammation and perivascular infiltration were absent in all recovery animals, indicating complete reversibility of inflammatory components.

**Liver:**

Groups 1 and 2: No treatment-related hepatic findings.

Group 3: Minimal hepatocellular vacuolisation (Grade 1) was observed in 3 of 10 males (M302, M305, M309). The finding consisted of small clear vacuoles within hepatocytes in the centrilobular region, consistent with glycogen or lipid accumulation. This finding was considered treatment-related but non-adverse at this dose based on minimal severity and restricted distribution.

Group 4: Mild hepatocellular vacuolisation (Grade 1 to 2) was present in all 10 males and 7 of 10 females. Distribution was centrilobular with diffuse involvement in the more severely affected animals. The finding is considered adverse in the context of the overall toxicological profile at 150 mg/kg/day.

Group 4R (Recovery): No residual hepatic vacuolisation was observed in any Group 4R recovery animal, indicating complete reversibility of this finding.

**Thymus:**

Groups 1 and 2: No treatment-related thymic findings.

Group 3: No treatment-related thymic findings.

Group 4: Thymic lymphoid depletion (Grade 1 to 2) was observed in 7 of 10 males and 6 of 10 females, characterised by decreased lymphocyte density in the cortex. This finding is consistent with stress-related involution secondary to body weight reduction and systemic toxicity.

Group 4R (Recovery): Residual mild thymic lymphoid depletion (Grade 1) was present in 1 of 5 Group 4R males. All other recovery animals showed complete thymic recovery.

**Other Organs (Kidneys, Heart, Brain, Spleen, Adrenal Glands, Gonads, Bone Marrow):**

No treatment-related microscopic findings were identified in the kidneys, heart, brain, spleen, adrenal glands, gonads, or bone marrow in any dose group or in recovery animals.

#### 5.3.4 Conclusion

The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) for Compound LF-892 under the conditions of this study is **10 mg/kg/day**, based on the absence of adverse findings at the low dose level (the minimal intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation observed in 2 of 10 Group 2 males was considered incidental and non-adverse). The lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL) is **50 mg/kg/day**, based on treatment-related intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation in all Group 3 males and the majority of Group 3 females.

The primary target organ of toxicity is the **lung**. The liver and thymus are secondary targets at 150 mg/kg/day. Lung macrophage accumulation was partially reversible following a 4-week recovery period; the inflammatory component was fully reversible. Hepatocellular vacuolisation and thymic lymphoid depletion were fully reversible during the recovery period.
