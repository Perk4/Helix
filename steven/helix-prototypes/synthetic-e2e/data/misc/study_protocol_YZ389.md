---

## 2. OBJECTIVES

The objectives of this study are to:

1. Determine the potential toxicity of Compound YZ-389 when administered orally to Sprague-Dawley rats for 28 consecutive days.
2. Characterise the nature, severity, and dose-response relationship of any treatment-related findings.
3. Identify potential target organs of toxicity based on macroscopic and microscopic anatomic pathology evaluation.
4. Establish a no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) to support further development.

## 10. ADMINISTRATION OF COMPOUND YZ-389

### 10.1 Route of Administration

Because Compound YZ-389 is intended for oral administration in humans, the same route will be used in this study.

### 10.2 Dose Selection

Dose levels for this study were selected based on data from a 7-day dose range-finding study (Protocol TOX-2024-DRF-004). In that study, Compound YZ-389 was administered orally to Sprague-Dawley rats at dose levels of 25, 100, and 400 mg/kg/day. The high dose of 400 mg/kg/day produced body weight reduction and clinical signs including reduced activity and soft stool. Liver weights were elevated at 400 mg/kg/day, suggesting hepatic involvement.

Based on these data, the high dose of 400 mg/kg/day has been selected to induce discernible adverse effects. The mid dose of 100 mg/kg/day is expected to produce minimal to moderate effects. The low dose of 25 mg/kg/day is anticipated to represent a NOAEL candidate.

### 10.3 Drug Administration

Dosing formulations of vehicle or Compound YZ-389 will be administered to rats by oral gavage once daily for 28 consecutive days. The dose volume will be adjusted based on the most recent body weight recorded for each animal. Animals will be dosed at approximately the same time each day (within a ±1 hour window).

### 10.4 Experimental Design

**Table 10.4-1: Experimental Design**

| Group Number | Treatment | Daily Dose (mg/kg) | Dose Volume (mL/kg) | Concentration (mg/mL) | Number of Animals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vehicle Control | 0 | 10 | 0 | 5 M, 5 F |
| 2 | YZ-389 Low Dose | 25 | 10 | 2.5 | 5 M, 5 F |
| 3 | YZ-389 Mid Dose | 100 | 10 | 10 | 5 M, 5 F |
| 4 | YZ-389 High Dose | 400 | 10 | 40 | 5 M, 5 F |

The following observations and tests will be conducted on all available animals unless otherwise noted. Additional observations may be performed at the discretion of the Study Director.

All animals will be observed for mortality and morbidity at least twice daily (morning and afternoon) from the first day of the pretest period and throughout the study. Moribund animals will be euthanised humanely and subjected to a full unscheduled necropsy.

| Timepoint | Day |
|---|---|

## 12. ANALYSIS OF RESULTS

**Continuous Variables (body weight, organ weights, food consumption):**
Mean values from treated groups will be compared to those of the control group using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett's test for multiple comparisons. Where Bartlett's test indicates heterogeneity of variance (p < 0.05), Kruskal-Wallis non-parametric ANOVA with Dunn's post-hoc test will be applied. Statistical significance is defined as p < 0.05 (two-tailed).

**Incidence Data (clinical observations, histopathology findings):**
Group comparisons will be made using Fisher's exact test where applicable. Qualitative findings (gross pathology, clinical assessment) will be compared using appropriate non-statistical methods.

Biological and toxicological significance will be assessed by the Study Director and Study Pathologist independent of statistical outcome. A statistically significant difference will not automatically be considered adverse, and a non-statistically significant difference will not be disregarded if it is considered biologically meaningful.
