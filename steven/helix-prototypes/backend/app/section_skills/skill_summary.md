# Skill: Summary Section
# Sources: approved_report_1, approved_report_2, approved_report_3

## Purpose
The Summary is the first section a reviewer reads. It must stand alone — a reader who reads only the Summary should understand the study design, key findings, and NOAEL/LOAEL without reading anything else.

## Structure (always in this order)
1. One sentence: compound, route, species, dose range, duration
2. One sentence: survival (or mortality if applicable)
3. One to two sentences: what was clean (lower doses or no findings at all)
4. One to two sentences: what was found at the adverse dose(s) — findings named specifically
5. One sentence: NOAEL. One sentence: LOAEL if established.

## Required Elements
- Compound name
- Route of administration
- Species
- Dose range (lowest to highest, including 0)
- Duration in plain English ("28 consecutive days", "13 consecutive weeks")
- Total animal count and group size
- Survival statement
- Named findings at each affected dose — not "findings were observed" but what the findings were
- NOAEL stated as a number with units
- LOAEL stated if established; "No LOAEL was established" if not

## What Must Not Be Missing
- Never omit NOAEL — it is the primary purpose of the study
- Never use "various findings" or "several effects" — name them
- Never state body weight changes without the magnitude and direction
- Always name which organs are affected

## Length
3 to 5 paragraphs. Each paragraph: 2 to 4 sentences. Summary is not a detailed findings section — it reports conclusions with enough specificity to be unambiguous.

## Example (from approved_report_2)
"Compound LF-892 was administered once daily by oral gavage to Sprague-Dawley rats (10/sex/group main cohort; 5/sex/group for control and high-dose recovery cohorts) at doses of 0, 10, 50, or 150 mg/kg/day for 13 consecutive weeks.

One unscheduled death occurred: male M101 was found moribund on Day 35 and euthanised. Post-mortem examination revealed oesophageal perforation consistent with a gavage error. This death is considered incidental to treatment and not compound-related.

No treatment-related effects were observed at 10 mg/kg/day. At 50 mg/kg/day, minimal pulmonary macrophage accumulation was observed in 2 of 10 males; this finding was considered non-adverse and incidental. At 150 mg/kg/day, treatment-related adverse findings included moderate to marked pulmonary inflammation with macrophage accumulation, hepatic vacuolisation, and thymic lymphoid depletion. Body weight was reduced at 150 mg/kg/day (−15.0% males, −9.8% females at Week 13).

The NOAEL is 10 mg/kg/day. The LOAEL is 50 mg/kg/day."
